## 1. Search Protocol and Methodology

### 1.1 Search Strings
The literature search employed Boolean combinations of keywords across three core thematic axes:
- **Axis 1 (Social IoT & Service Discovery):** `"Social Internet of Things"` OR `"SIoT"` AND (`"service discovery"` OR `"service composition"` OR `"routing"` OR `"path selection"`).
- **Axis 2 (Trust Management & Security):** `"trust management"` OR `"reputation"` AND (`"direct trust"` OR `"indirect trust"` OR `"recommendation"` OR `"collusion"` OR `"bad mouthing"` OR `"ballot stuffing"` OR `"entropy"`).
- **Axis 3 (Optimization Metaheuristics):** `"Dung Beetle Optimizer"` OR `"DBO"` OR `"Marine Predators Algorithm"` OR `"MPA"` OR `"multi-objective"` OR `"Pareto"` OR `"hybrid metaheuristic"`.

### 1.2 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  1. Peer-reviewed journal articles, premier conference proceedings, and verified foundational book chapters published between 2012 and 2026.
  2. Studies addressing trust modeling in decentralized, social, or IoT environments.
  3. Research on nature-inspired metaheuristic algorithms applied to discrete, multi-objective, or combinatorial routing/service selection problems.
  4. Studies containing formal mathematical models, explicit algorithmic descriptions, and reproducible experimental evaluations.
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  1. Non-peer-reviewed preprints without verifiable publication records.
  2. Generic survey articles that do not propose an operational model or empirical benchmark.
  3. Studies relying exclusively on centralized deep learning, reinforcement learning, or federated learning without transparent metaheuristic optimization.
  4. Papers with undefined mathematical formulations or unverified commercial simulation tools.

## 3. Critical Research-Gap Synthesis

A rigorous synthesis of the existing literature reveals five critical deficiencies in the state-of-the-art:

1. **Absence of Unified DBO-MPA Hybridization for Discrete Graph Routing:**
   While DBO excels in multi-phase exploratory divergence (via ball-rolling, dancing, and breeding) and MPA excels in multi-regime velocity exploitation (via Brownian motion, Lévy walks, and FADs perturbations), both algorithms were fundamentally designed for continuous, unconstrained optimization benchmarks. No existing study has successfully combined DBO's population diversity with MPA's scale-free Lévy exploitation for combinatorial graph path discovery in Social IoT.

2. **Vulnerability of Trust Models to Coordinated Collusion and Entropy Manipulation:**
   Prior trust models in SIoT rely either on direct Laplace/Beta smoothing or simple weighted recommendation averaging. Existing recommendation filtering techniques (such as standard deviation or basic Shannon entropy) assume that malicious recommendations exhibit high distributional variance. Consequently, sophisticated collusion syndicates that submit coordinated, low-variance fraudulent ratings easily bypass conventional anomaly detectors.

3. **Decoupling of Social Topologies from Physical Network Feasibility:**
   Extant SIoT trust frameworks frequently treat social connections (e.g. friendship or community ties) as equivalent to operational wireless links. Conversely, traditional IoT routing optimizers focus exclusively on physical QoS (latency, energy, hop count) while completely ignoring device trustworthiness. This disconnect produces service paths that are either physically unroutable or dangerously compromised by untrustworthy relays.

4. **Predominance of Single-Objective Scalarization over True Pareto Multi-Objective Optimization:**
   The overwhelming majority of existing trust-aware routing algorithms collapse trust, latency, and energy into an arbitrary weighted-sum scalar fitness function. This conceals critical trade-offs, requires subjective *a priori* weighting, and fails to discover the multi-dimensional Pareto-optimal frontier essential for heterogeneous IoT applications with varying latency and security budgets.

5. **Lack of Controlled Experimental Benchmarking and Reproducibility:**
   Many previous SIoT studies rely on unverified, proprietary, or mischaracterized datasets (such as the unverified "San Francisco 14,000-device SIoT dataset"), conduct fewer than 10 stochastic repetitions, omit rigorous statistical tests (e.g. Wilcoxon signed-rank with family-wise error control, Friedman tests), and fail to provide open-source reproducible implementations.
